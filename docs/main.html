Sports technology at UT Dallas

Not a single center: a cross-department network tied to teaching and startups.

Teach

  • MS in Systems Engineering and Management: Sports Technology Systems concentration (on campus and online)
  • Analytics, wearables, athlete performance, fan experience

Research

  • 8 faculty across 5 departments: Bioengineering, Computer Science, Electrical & Computer Eng., Materials Science, Mechanical Eng.
  • Movement, AI coaching, wearables, sleep and recovery, materials

Translate

  • UTDesign: every engineering and CS senior completes a company-sponsored capstone
  • Institute for Innovation and Entrepreneurship, Venture Development Center, faculty spinouts

~45 s. Frame: sports tech at UTD is distributed rather than housed in one institute. The concentration answers their interest in teaching; the rest of the talk is the research and the translation pathways.

The athlete in motion

Gu Eon Kang Active
Bioengineering · Neuromuscular & Musculoskeletal Biomechanics Lab

Baseball biomechanics with UT Dallas Baseball and DFW training centers. Return-to-sport jump/landing analysis after ACL reconstruction with Texas Health Resources Sports Medicine.

Liang He Active
Computer Science

AgentCoach (CHI 2026): AI that turns tutorial videos into movement evaluation and adaptive coaching feedback, validated on sports and exercise videos.

Hao Zhou Emerging
Computer Science · Human-Aware Intelligence Lab

Full-body muscle activation from insole pressure sensors (MobiCom 2026); smart rings, earbuds, physiological sensing. Athlete monitoring is a natural next step.

Nasser Kehtarnavaz Earlier work
Electrical & Computer Eng. · Embedded Machine Learning Lab

UTD-MHAD (2015): camera + wearable inertial sensing that recognizes basketball shots, baseball swings, tennis strokes, squats.

Tags: Active: current sports/athlete project · Emerging: closely relevant new technology · Earlier work: documented past project

~1 min. Kang is the most direct: real teams, real clinical partner. He = AI coaching. Zhou just arrived; his insole work is directly relevant even though no athlete partnership yet. Kehtarnavaz = the sensing/recognition methods base.

Recovery, physiology, and equipment

Matthew Walker Active
Bioengineering & Neuroscience · Sleep Innovation Labs

Sleep measurement and intervention tools for health and professional-athlete performance, including a brain-stimulation headband used by NBA players.

Shalini Prasad Active
Department Head, Bioengineering · Biomedical Microdevices & Nanotechnology Lab

Wearable sweat biosensors: continuous cortisol and melatonin (2026). Co-founder of EnLiSense. Advised a 2016 capstone hydration wearable for athletes.

Walter Voit Active
Materials Science & Mechanical Eng. · Director, Center for Engineering Innovation

3D-printed elastomers for running-shoe midsoles: lighter, more comfortable, more energy return. Commercialized through his spinout Adaptive3D.

Hongbing Lu Earlier work
Mechanical Engineering · Mechanics of Advanced Materials Lab

Experimental mechanics and dynamic material behavior. Supported a 2014 capstone modeling sports-related mild traumatic brain injury.

~1 min. Walker and Prasad: recovery and physiology, both with commercial ties. Voit: equipment, and the clearest commercialization story (next slide). Lu: impact/injury mechanics, an earlier lead.

From lab to market

Spinout → acquisition

Adaptive3D (Voit): tough 3D-printable rubbers for footwear to aerospace. Launched with UT Dallas’s Venture Development Center; acquired by Desktop Metal in 2021.

Faculty-founded company

EnLiSense (Prasad, co-founder): non-invasive sweat-biomarker wearables; its CORTI platform was validated in her UT Dallas lab.

Founder-scientist

Walker brings several neurotechnology startups; his sleep-stimulation headband is already in professional sport.

Students as innovators

UTDesign capstones are proposed and mentored by companies. Example: a student team’s athlete hydration wearable (2016).

~1 min. This is the slide for an innovation & entrepreneurship audience: four distinct pathways from research to product.
